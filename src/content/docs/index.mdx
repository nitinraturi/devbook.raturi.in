  <ol class="dvb-flow-steps">
    <li>
      <span class="dvb-flow-num" aria-hidden="true">1</span>
      <h3>Reads, writes nothing</h3>
      <p>Finds your agent, your stack and the format, lint, typecheck, test and build commands your repository already uses, and runs each once.</p>
    </li>
    <li>
      <span class="dvb-flow-num" aria-hidden="true">2</span>
      <h3>Shows a plan and waits</h3>
      <p>One table: each file it would write, each skill with its source and pinned version, each hook. Nothing happens until you say yes.</p>
    </li>
    <li>
      <span class="dvb-flow-num" aria-hidden="true">3</span>
      <h3>Merges, never overwrites</h3>
      <p>Writes the Devbook block into <code>AGENTS.md</code> between markers and keeps every line you wrote. Claude Code gets <code>@AGENTS.md</code> in <code>CLAUDE.md</code>.</p>
    </li>
    <li>
      <span class="dvb-flow-num" aria-hidden="true">4</span>
      <h3>Installs a little, pinned</h3>
      <p>At most three skills from vendor-maintained sources, read in full before install. It records everything in <code>devbook.lock.json</code> and leaves the commit to you.</p>
    </li>
  </ol>
</section>

    <div>
      <p class="dvb-home-eyebrow">The only always-loaded part</p>
      <h2 id="result-title">The core block</h2>
    </div>
  </div>
  <p>These lines go into your <code>AGENTS.md</code>, followed by your repository's real commands. They cover only what agents get wrong without being told: proving work with real output, staying out of commits and deploys, asking before risky changes, and keeping secrets out.</p>
  <Code code={core} lang="md" title="AGENTS.md (Devbook block)" />
  <div class="dvb-home-layer-grid">
    <article>
      <h3>Never touched</h3>
      <p>CI and deploy config, git hooks, branch protection, <code>.env</code> files and your own instructions. The prompt reads CI only to learn your commands.</p>
    </article>
    <article>
      <h3>Enforced where possible</h3>
      <p>In Claude Code, permission rules block <code>git push</code> and reads of <code>.env</code>, and a hook stops edits to CI and deploy paths. A hook runs every time; a line of text is only advice.</p>
    </article>
    <article>
      <h3>Yours wins</h3>
      <p>Your request beats your project rules, and your project rules beat the Devbook block. Put your own rules under "Project rules" in <code>AGENTS.md</code>.</p>
    </article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="dvb-home-daily" aria-labelledby="why-title">
  <div class="dvb-home-section-heading">
    <div>
      <p class="dvb-home-eyebrow">Curated, not written</p>
      <h2 id="why-title">Why it is this small</h2>
    </div>
    <a class="dvb-home-text-link" href="/catalog/">The catalog <span aria-hidden="true">→</span></a>
  </div>
  <div class="dvb-flow-tiers">
    <article>
      <p class="dvb-flow-tier-label">Context files</p>
      <h3>Only what agents can't discover</h3>
      <p>Hand-written context files help when they hold facts the agent can't find on its own. Generated ones lowered success rates and raised cost by about 20% (<a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988v1">arXiv 2602.11988</a>).</p>
    </article>
    <article>
      <p class="dvb-flow-tier-label">Skills</p>
      <h3>Two or three, not ten</h3>
      <p>Curated skills help, but four or more cut most of the gain, and software engineering gains least of any domain (<a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670v1">SkillsBench</a>). Hence the cap of three.</p>
    </article>
    <article>
      <p class="dvb-flow-tier-label">Stack knowledge</p>
      <h3>From the vendor</h3>
      <p>Cloudflare, Expo, Stripe, Supabase and others now maintain their own skills. Devbook pins them instead of writing its own copies.</p>
    </article>
    <article>
      <p class="dvb-flow-tier-label">Supply chain</p>
      <h3>Pinned and read first</h3>
      <p>Public skill registries have shipped malicious skills (<a href="https://snyk.io/blog/toxicskills-malicious-ai-agent-skills-clawhub/">Snyk</a>). The prompt installs only from an allowlist, pinned, after reading every file.</p>
    </article>
  </div>
  <p class="dvb-skill-note">The evidence that any of this improves code is modest. Devbook measures its own effect monthly against no Devbook at all, and publishes the result. <a href="/evals/">The evals</a></p>
</section>
